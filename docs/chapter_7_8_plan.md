# Chapters 7 and 8: ideas to test

**Status (2026-10-16): the user picked 7A, Aries, the ram's charge, and 8A, Taurus, the red cloth, from the shortlist below. Both are at step A (paper). Nothing is built or committed.** This follows the chapters 4-6 process ([chapter_4_6_plan.md](chapter_4_6_plan.md)): each pick passes gates A-D one experiment at a time, Aries first, and every design guess is flagged. [design.md](design.md) is still the rules reference.

How the shortlist was made: a design panel of six independent proposers, each with its own lens (myth, slot-machine payoff, stakes, minimal screen, a new pressure axis, engine reuse), produced 18 proposals. These were merged into 11 distinct candidates, and each was attacked by three judges (stakes, feel and clarity, fit and build cost; 1-5 each). The scores were low across the board (the best was 9/15, and none scored above 3 on feel), so every option is a candidate for the gates, not a finished design. The alternatives stay below for reference.

**Defaults taken for the open questions until the user says otherwise (flagged):** 7A's charge runs after the burst (stronger stakes; the aim preview then shows the lane, not where fresh stars will be pushed), and its lane follows the row of the landmark the last link lit. The payout spikes (GOLDEN FLEECE, MATADOR, the zeta supernova) each wait for their own approval.

## 1. Direction and stakes

| Chapter | Pressure on | The decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Scorpio | **who** takes your stars (Orion, an enemy) | protect the stars a landmark needs |
| 2 Aquarius | **where** they are (the current, the drain) | link a cluster now, or let the flow bring a better one and risk it |
| 3 Leo | **what** they are (the heat, sizes) | link now, or let it ripen and risk the burn |
| 4 Virgo | **when** they pay (the harvest, bound sheaves) | which landmark to light first, before the scythe |
| 5 Sagittarius | **where** they fall (the black hole) | link before the pull takes them, or aim into orbit and wait |
| 6 Pisces | **how** you aim and which half you keep (the reflection, the wave) | where to throw a mirrored burst, and which half to cash in before the wave |
| 7 Aries | **where your link points the threat** (the ram's charge) | which landmark to light next, since the ram charges its row |
| 8 Taurus | **which combo** you make (the red cloth) | a sequence now (it angers the bull) or a triple (it calms it), and when to let it charge |

Neither the recommended pair nor any alternative is a drift, a pull, a size change, a clock or a half-sky wipe. Every option states up front why linking first doesn't save you. That is the structural flaw that sank Leo's part stages, Virgo's paying harvest and the cords.

## 2. Shortlist

The judges' scores (stakes / feel / fit, 1-5 each) are for the candidate **as first proposed**. The rules below already include the judges' main fixes, so treat the scores as a floor and the "main risk" line as what the gates must answer.

### Chapter 7: Aries, the ram (+1 sign east of Pisces)

#### 7A. The ram's charge: the landmark you light aims it *(picked)*
*Judges 2/3/3 = 8 (2 fatal flaws, both addressed below). Built from A1, with A5 and T4 folded in.*

- **Rule.** The ram's head stands at one sky edge, and two hoof pips under it count down launches. Every 2 launches it charges a horizontal lane 20 px tall, centred on the row of **the landmark your last link lit** (before any link: the starting lit star). You know the row before you trace, so there is no trace-order bookkeeping.
  - Loose stars in the lane are butted 48 px along it. A butted star that runs into a landmark stops against it and is **fleeced** (a gold outline ring; the body colour never changes). A star that no landmark stops goes off the far edge and is lost.
  - The charge runs **after** the charge launch's burst lands, so fresh stars that land in the lane are hit too. That is the stakes judge's fix. *Design guess to flag:* before-burst keeps an exact aim preview but scores 2-3 on stakes.
- **Real loss.** Stars butted off the edge are lost for nothing. Usually that is the waiting pair for the next landmark, which sits in the row you just lit, or a fresh twin. On stage 5 and in the final the butt is full width, and a charge can take the last traceable combo, which loses the run.
- **Why link-first loses.** Linking aims the ram. The next landmark sits beside the one just lit, so its waiting pair shares the lit row, and stage layouts seed it there (Aries's back is a horizontal chain). Link-first charges its own pair.
- **Why hoarding loses.** With no new link, the lane stays where it was, and the charge still comes every 2 launches. A crowded sky fills every row, so each charge butts 3-6 stars, and from stage 4 the sides alternate.
- **On screen (busiest aim): 4 cues.** The ram's head and pips, two dotted rails, ember rings on stars that would be lost, and gold rings on fleeced stars. While a link is traced, the rails jump to the row of the landmark that link would light. No rest brackets and no equinox ring. Hoof dust and sparks, not water.
- **Opening demo (about 3 s, demo stars only).** A ghost link lights a demo landmark, and the rails snap to its row. The pips tick, the ram rears for 2 frames, and hooves thunder. One lane star is butted into the landmark and turns gold. A second is butted off the edge in sparks.
- **Payout spike.** **GOLDEN FLEECE.** A link using fleeced stars pays +4 each (capped per link). When one charge fleeces 3 or more stars, the horns flash and a GOLDEN FLEECE +N card shows. You leave stars in the lane on purpose and bet the figure stops them. Needs its own approval.
- **Ladder.**
  1. HORNS: a fixed lane on the lit star's row, every 3 launches, a 32 px butt, and stars stop at the edge (no loss). It teaches herding into the figure. Link-first is safe on purpose.
  2. HEAD: the loss. The lane follows the landmark you lit, butt 48, every 2 launches.
  3. FLEECE/BACK: the back is a long wall, and the fleece pays.
  4. LEGS: the sides alternate, shown by the head swapping edges, so no side of a landmark shelters for long.
  5. TAIL, Helle's fall: from launch 3 the ram charges every launch at full width, and only the figure holds stars.
- **Final: ARIES, THE GOLDEN RAM** (11-12 stars, title card).
  - Act one: the charge follows the landmark you lit, from alternating sides, and the fleece pays.
  - Act two, the equinox: on launch 6 the year turns, telegraphed by the hoof pips turning ember. From then on the ram charges every launch at full width.
  - Climax: the winning link triggers the last charge, which sweeps every row top to bottom in hard steps. Every leftover is butted into the figure, gilds and bursts for dust under GOLDEN FLEECE +N. The threat becomes the jackpot.
- **Main risk.** It is a cousin of the Pisces wave (a telegraphed sweep). It differs because you aim it and it pushes rather than erases, but you should judge that in play. Stakes against link-first depend on the after-burst timing and on layouts that put waiting pairs in lit rows. The bots must show it.

#### 7B. Head-first: your link's last step butts the figure
*Judges 2/2/3 = 7 (3 fatal flaws). Built from A4, with T1's herd folded in, so a herd version is not offered separately.*

- **Rule.** Every successful link ends with the ram butting from the last traced star along the last step, snapped to 8 directions.
  - It strikes the first thing within 64 px on that line. A **lit landmark goes dark** (the main threat, per the judges' fix). A loose star is knocked 32 px on and lost if it leaves the sky.
  - From stage 3, if no link is made for 2 launches, the ram butts on its own from the last lit landmark.
  - Combo validity never changes. An invalid link butts nothing.
- **Real loss.** Lit landmarks go dark (progress lost for nothing), and loose stars are knocked out of the sky.
- **Why link-first loses.** Each part is laid out so the stars that complete combos sit on the far side of lit landmarks. Linking as soon as a combo exists points the ram back into the figure. Skill means waiting for, or throwing toward, a combo whose end star lets you trace away.
- **Why hoarding loses.** The impatience butt fires into a crowding sky, and a packed sky leaves no clear direction.
- **On screen: 3 cues, all while tracing only.** One dotted ray from the third star, the ember ring or two ember dots on whatever it hits, and the horn glyph by Hamal lowering when an impatience butt is due. No cannon chain, no cracks, no both-ends stage.
- **Opening demo.** Small, medium and big demo stars are traced so the ray points at a demo landmark, which goes dark with a thud. The same three are traced in reverse, the ray points at empty sky, and nothing is lost. Rule and escape are shown in one take.
- **Payout spike.** **THE RAM'S RUN** in the final only: the winning link's ray runs the whole sky and leftovers burst for dust.
- **Ladder.**
  1. HORNS: a gentle butt that only nudges loose stars, so you learn to herd. Safe on purpose.
  2. HEAD: lit landmarks go dark.
  3. FLEECE: the impatience butt.
  4. LEGS: reach 80 px, and the legs hang along easy tracing directions.
  5. TAIL: the clash, where a knocked star that hits another loose star cracks both.
- **Final: THE YEAR TURNS.** The arrival charge crosses the figure. All rules are on. The climax is the Ram's Run along the winning line.
- **Main risk.** A safe trace order usually exists among the 6 orders, which makes this the "dodge the geometry" chore you rejected with the cords. Its strength is the strongest contrast with 8A: instant, no timer, no gauge.

#### 7C. The golden fleece: waiting pays
*Judges 2/2/2 = 6 (3 fatal flaws). Built from A3 and rebuilt. Offered because "waiting pays" is the campaign's biggest open hole. It is the weakest option.*

- **Rule.** The fleece is **one warm object** carried by your throw. When a planet bursts, the loose stars already within 24 px of the impact are gilded.
  - A link of 3 gilded stars is a GOLDEN FLEECE: +10 dust and +1 light.
  - Every 3rd launch, telegraphed by Jason's ship crossing the top, the fleece is raided. Every gilded star still loose is taken, and every landmark lit by a link with **no** gilded star goes dark.
- **Real loss.** Gilded stars that were hoarded, and landmarks lit "cheaply".
- **Why link-first loses.** Its plain links don't hold their landmarks through a raid.
- **Why hoarding loses.** The raid takes the gold it was saving.
- **On screen: 3 cues.** The fleece, gold rings on gilded stars, and the ship's countdown.
- **Payout spike.** The golden trio: "one more throw for the gold?"
- **Main risk.** The raid's reap-and-unlight is close to Virgo's scythe and bound sheaves. Ageing variants were judged a reskin of Leo's heat. Only take it if you value the push-your-luck enough to fight that.

*Also playable as Aries:* **8B's throw-aimed charge** ("the ram's run"). It is listed once, under chapter 8.

### Chapter 8: Taurus, the bull (+1 sign east of Aries, standing over Orion)

#### 8A. The red cloth: sequences anger the bull, triples calm it *(picked)*
*Judges 4/2/3 = 9 (1 fatal flaw: feel, addressed by the cuts below). Built from T2, trimmed to one rule.*

- **Rule.** The bull's rage is one gauge (max 5), shown on the painting's horn and its lowering head.
  - Each launch adds 1. A one-of-each **sequence** waves the red cloth: a warm 2-frame flick on the link, +1. A **triple** calms the bull: its collect particles fly into Aldebaran's eye, −2.
  - At max−1 the eye locks a target, shown a full turn ahead: the densest 48 px cluster of loose stars. If that cluster holds fewer than 2 stars, the target is the nearest lit landmark (from stage 2).
  - At max the bull charges on the next launch, before the burst: a StarSplash at the target throws stars off the sky, and a lit landmark within 16 px is gored dark. Rage then resets to 2.
- **Cut from the original:** the crowd pip, the eye cycling sizes, red packs adding rage, the dust per pip, the base dust cut and the matador doubling.
- **Real loss.** A whole cluster thrown off the sky, or a lit landmark gored dark. Two charges can end a tight stage.
- **Why link-first loses.** Mixed bursts make sequences the most common combo, so link-first fills the gauge about 2 pips per throw. In a thin sky the bull gores the landmark it just lit.
- **Why hoarding loses.** The gauge fills from launches anyway, and the charge lands on the biggest cluster, which is the hoard. A triples-only policy also loses, because it strands mixed stars, so the answer is judgement rather than a fixed policy.
- **On screen: 3-4 cues.** The bull's posture as the gauge, the locked target ring (only at max−1), ember rings or two dots on what it would hit, and the gauge blink while a link is traced.
- **Opening demo (about 3.5 s).** A ghost sequence links, the cloth flicks, and the head lowers a step. The gauge fills, the eye locks on a demo clump, and the bull charges with a 2-frame streak and a crash flash, knocking the clump off the edge. A demo triple then shuts the eye with a cool puff.
- **Payout spike.** **MATADOR.** A triple linked while the gauge is full makes the bull pass: a 3-frame hard stop mid-lunge, the cape snapping across the sky, +8. It is pure slot tension: you hold the pair, wait, and risk the charge. Needs its own approval.
- **Ladder.**
  1. HORNS: only launches fill rage, there is no gore, and link-first is safe on purpose.
  2. EYE (Aldebaran): sequences anger and triples calm. Empty-sky gores hit the nearest lit landmark. The intro plays here.
  3. HYADES: the matador pass.
  4. PLEIADES (one cluster landmark sprite): gores hit any lit landmark near the target.
  5. FORELEGS, the rodeo: after a charge rage resets to 4, so the bull comes back fast.
- **Final: THE CRAB NEBULA** (13 stars, the Pleiades as one landmark, title card TAURUS).
  - The arrival opens Aldebaran's eye.
  - Act two: zeta Tauri, the south horn tip beside M1, gains a pixel ring for each charge you survive.
  - Climax: lighting zeta last with rage at 4 or more sends the horn tip **supernova** (1054). The rewarding big_bang_finale is centred on zeta and swallows every leftover for dust × rage. With a calm bull you win through the ordinary finale. The bull's rage becomes the jackpot, the Sagittarius pattern.
- **Main risk.** The combo choice only bites when both a triple and a sequence can be traced, so the bots must measure that share first. If it is low, vary the size odds per stage. The gauge is also close to Sagittarius's, though here links lower it and a full gauge pays.

#### 8B. The cape: your throw aims the next charge
*Judges 2/2/2 = 6 for T3 and A5. Rebuilt from both with T3's fix. Could be Aries instead ("the ram's run").*

- **Rule.** The bull stands at a sky edge and paws once per throw. The paw marks are on its body, and from stage 3 links paw too.
  - On every 3rd paw it charges in a straight line from its head to **your previous throw's impact point** (the cape).
  - Loose stars within 7 px of the line are tossed off the sky. From stage 4, lit landmarks on the line go dark.
  - The line is drawn a full turn before the charge, so you can still link what is on it. While aiming the throw that sets the cape, one dotted line previews it.
- **Real loss.** The stars of your previous burst, which lie on the line by construction, and lit landmarks on the line.
- **Why link-first loses.** Links bring the charge sooner, and the cape sits on your newest stars.
- **Why hoarding loses.** In a crowded sky every line crosses stars.
- **On screen: 3-4 cues.** The bull with its paw marks, one dotted line, ember rings on stars, and dots on a landmark.
- **Payout spike.** **OLE**: a charge down an empty line pays a small bonus with a gold trail. The final's GREAT CHARGE is aimed through the leftovers.
- **Main risk.** The stakes depend on aiming, the weakest kind in past chapters. "Aim so the line misses" may feel like the cords chore. It also collides with 7A (two aimed charges), so pair it with 7B or 7C, not 7A.

#### 8C. The bull run: unlit landmarks are walls you tear down
*Judges 2/3/2 = 7 (3 fatal flaws). Built from T5 and rebuilt with the stakes and fit fixes.*

- **Rule.** Every 3rd throw, telegraphed a turn ahead by a stamp cue, the bull charges a known row in a fixed, shown order.
  - Loose stars in the row are carried along it. A star that piles against an **unlit** landmark stops and is safe. Every other star in the row is trampled.
  - Lit landmarks give no shelter.
  - A pile of 3 against one landmark that forms a valid combo is **GORED**: it links to that landmark automatically for ×2.
- **Real loss.** Stars caught in the open are trampled.
- **Why link-first loses.** Lighting a landmark removes its shelter, so link-first tears down its own walls and then loses its waiting pairs.
- **Why hoarding loses.** Piles overflow, and the 4th star crushes the pile.
- **On screen: 3-4 cues.** The bull and the row, ember rings on stars in the open, and horn marks on unlit walls.
- **Main risk.** A rhythmic row sweep sits close to the wave. It overlaps 7A (both stop stars against landmarks), so never pair 7A with 8C.

## 3. The pair: 7A Aries, the ram's charge, then 8A Taurus, the red cloth

- **The two contrast with each other.**
  - In Aries, **where you light** aims the threat. It is spatial: the order you light landmarks in matters, and the threat is a push along a row. The figure is a **shelter** that gilds stars.
  - In Taurus, **which combo you make** feeds the threat. It is a paytable choice, and the threat is a gauge plus a point impact on the densest cluster. The figure is a **target** that gets gored.
  - The charges look different too: a sideways butt along a row against a splash ring on a cluster.
- **They contrast with chapters 1-6.** Neither is a drift (2), a pull (5), a size change (3), a clock (4) or a half wipe (6). Both act **on links**, which is the only family that has ever broken link-first: Leo's breath, Virgo's binding and Sagittarius's gauge.
  - 8A opens the one axis no chapter has touched: triple vs sequence.
  - 7A makes the order you light landmarks change where the hazard goes.
- **Payout and story.** Each one turns its threat into the final's jackpot (the GOLDEN FLEECE sweep, the zeta supernova), the pattern you praised in Sagittarius. The voyage runs straight east: Pisces, Aries, Taurus.
- **Fallback.** If two charging animals in a row feel samey in play, swap 7A for **7B (head-first)**. It has no timer and no gauge, so it contrasts most with 8A. Don't pair 8B with 7A, or 8C with 7A.
