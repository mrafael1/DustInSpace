# Dust In Space: Game Design

Living design reference. Numbers live in `game/config/balance.json`, not here.

## Pillars

1. **Keep the slot-machine feelings, not the slot machine.** The game should deliver anticipation, randomness, small interventions and satisfying payouts without spinning reels.
2. **Tactile and frequent.** Every few seconds the player does something small (launch, link, collect) and the sky reacts visibly.
3. **One central loop before any systems.** No extra currencies, progression layers or special cases until the core loop is fun. This is the main lesson from the previous project.

## Core loop: Restore the Sun (first playable)

The player wins by giving the Sun its light back before running out of packs and dust. The Sun visibly brightens as the run progresses.

1. Start with packs from the balance file (2 blue, 1 red), 0 dust and a dark Sun.
2. Pull back a planet-shaped pack in the slingshot and release it into the sky.
3. The pack bursts where it lands, and its stars scatter and settle within the playable sky.
4. Link exactly 3 stars into a valid combination by dragging through them, or by tapping them one at a time.
5. A valid link lights up, then dissolves: dust particles fly to the dust counter and light particles fly into the Sun.
6. Spend dust on more packs. Unused stars stay in the sky.

### Stars

There are three sizes: **small, medium and big**. Size and silhouette are the only way to tell them apart; stars show no numbers or pips.

### Combinations

| Combination | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 small / 3 medium / 3 big | Triples: mostly dust, to keep buying packs |
| 1 small + 1 medium + 1 big, in **any order** | Sequence: a lot of light, the main way to restore the Sun |

- An invalid link cancels and uses nothing up.
- **Taking a link back (web playtest: a wrong link was gone the moment the finger lifted).** Dragging back onto the star before the last drops the last one, so a drag can be retraced. Once 3 stars are picked, a drag that leaves the last star (released off it and more than 16 px past where the finger last was on it) lets the link go: nothing is linked or used, the selection clears, and the cancel sound plays (the slingshot's). While the finger is out there, the whole line shows let go: sparse M5 dots like an out-of-reach step, not the warm line, so the player sees it before lifting. Sliding off the third star of a tapped link works the same way. With fewer than 3 picked, the finger is still looking for the next star, so a release anywhere is a short link: rejected with its shake, as before.
- Distance and crossings are ignored for now; spatial rules may come later.
- When a link is collected, its dust floats up from it as "+n" until the dust lands on the counter (prototype, #59). There is no preview while tracing, and the Sun shows its light by its fill, not a number.

### The table (#94)

A slot machine's paytable, titled **COMBOS**. The **COMBOS** button (the button plaque's style, top-right, under the pause button) opens it at any time in a run, and the tutorial opens it at its first link.

- A plaque (N0, N6 border) over the sky with one row per link: small, medium and big triples, then one of each size, all in the sky stars' own art on a dim link line (N4, behind them) so each row reads as a link. The one-of-each row cuts through **all six orders** in turn, 0.6 s each, since any order counts.
- One line of column heads: **COMBOS** centred over the stars, **DUST** and **LIGHT** over their columns.
- Each row shows its **dust** as a lavender number (D0, the dust's colour, right-aligned so the dust icons line up) and its **light** as tiny suns (7x7, all pale gold C1, a solid round centre and eight short rays, so they never read as sky stars), one per unit, left-aligned. The unit is the smallest light any link gives (shipped: 5), so the suns stay true when tuned: 1 / 2 / 3 / 5. A row shows at most 5 suns (what fits): if the most light would need more, the unit grows until it fits. Everything is read from `balance.json`.
- **TAP TO CLOSE** sits at the plaque's foot in the small 3x5 font. While the table is open, the tutorial's line and hand are hidden (the line would crowd the plaque's top) and come back when it closes.
- **The game holds still** while it's open: the sky, the launcher, Orion, the sequencer, the payouts and the HUD's own timers (messages, the tutorial's guide) all stop. Any tap closes it, and the touch does nothing else.

### Packs

| | Blue | Red |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Cheap and sustaining; favours small stars; one burst of 3 | Expensive; favours big stars; **twin burst**, 3 + 3 stars; slightly higher Big Bang chance |

- Each star is drawn independently from the pack's weights.
- **Red's twin burst:** one aim; the planet flies and trembles at the aim like any pack, then splits into twin planets that dart apart and burst `burst_spread` px apart across the aim (slid back together at a sky edge), each with `stars` stars. The aim previews both scatter rings. It's still one launch: one Big Bang roll (a red Big Bang splits too, then collapses at the aim, so it keeps its surprise), one count for Orion, and the hunting circle strikes once, after both bursts. Sound: the launch whoosh, a higher whoosh as it splits, a burst for each. Tuning: `packs.red.stars` (3), `bursts` (2), `burst_spread` (44: the two scatter rings sit side by side and stay within link reach of each other). The icon may change after playtesting.
- The slingshot controls **where** a pack bursts, never its contents.
- Buying a pack loads it into the launcher. The player chooses which owned pack to load.
- **A finger aims above itself (#152, phone playtest: the reticle, scatter ring and at-risk previews sat under the fingertip).** While a finger is on the sky, the telescope aims 28 px above it (clamped into the sky), so what the aim previews stays in sight; the finger sits 28 px under the sky's lowest row to aim there. Lifting launches at the reticle. **Let go:** sliding the finger back down to the telescope (so low that even lifted it would aim under the sky, or onto the telescope) shows the aim let go (only cold M5 brackets: no warm core, scatter ring or sight line) before lifting, and lifting there launches nothing: the cancel sound plays (the slingshot's, like a let-go link) and the telescope keeps aiming. A mouse aims right at the pointer as before (hovering aims, a click launches, no let-go). The guided run's launch hands (steps 1, 3 and red) point 28 px under the spot after a finger's press (or on a touch screen), where the finger goes; at a mouse, at the spot. The 28 px is input ergonomics (`Telescope.TOUCH_LIFT`), not balance.
- **A launch leaves the launcher empty** (web playtest: an automatic reload armed a planet the player hadn't picked, and in later stages they kept having to work around it). The next launch needs a tap on an icon (or a buy), which loads and aims as before. While it's empty the telescope doesn't aim, so touches on the sky pick stars; a tap on the empty telescope gets the refused buzz and its empty line. The run still starts with the first owned kind loaded. The guided first run keeps reloading until free play, since its scripted launches need their planet.
- In the HUD, tapping a pack's icon **loads** it if the player owns one (free, never a purchase), or **buys** it if they own none and can afford it.
- Under each icon, a **buy button** ("+◆cost") always buys one more, however many are owned. It's drawn as a small plate with a "+", so it reads as a button without being explained. Its border is warm when the dust is there and cool when it isn't. It lightens while pressed, flashes when the buy plays, and turns red with a refused tap (the icon nudges too). A buy charges the cost once, adds one pack and loads it.
- The icon is lit and spinning while at least one is owned, and grey and still at ×0, even when the dust would buy one (tapping it then buys the first; the buy button shows the dust is there). A lit icon hops now and then while the dust can buy another.
- Tap targets are 44 pt (22 native px) each: the icon's reaches up into the land strip, the button's runs to the bottom of the screen.

### Legendary opening: Big Bang

- Rolled once per pack, before any stars are drawn.
- The opening starts out looking normal. Then the pack ruptures, every star in the sky collapses inward, there is a short silent pause, and the Big Bang goes off.
- It clears all stars and awards base dust plus a bonus for each star cleared. It gives no light, and light already earned is kept.
- A development-only trigger forces it (key **B** in debug builds).
- **Off in the Scorpio chapter** (`scorpio.big_bang: false`): on a constellation stage no pack opens as a Big Bang. The flag covers every chapter's stages, so a player never meets it and the guided run doesn't teach it. That is a deliberate choice (#148), not a gap: it stays off until it has a home that can't wipe landmark progress. It gave no light and never touched the landmarks, so there it only wiped the stars saved for a landmark (with it the chapter's win rates were 2-5 points higher, without changing the shape of any stage; measured before the twin burst). The roll is still made, so packs open as they would with it on until a roll that would have come up: that pack draws its stars instead, and the packs after it differ; the debug trigger still forces one, and the plain Sun stage keeps it. The chapter's current simulator figures, all without it, are in the Balance snapshot.

### Win and loss

- **Win:** Sun light reaches the target. The Sun fully ignites and lights up the sky.
- **Loss:** the player has no packs left, not enough dust to buy one, **and** no valid combination remains in the sky. Always check for remaining combinations before ending the run.

### Scorpio chapter chart (#62)

The game opens on Scorpio's chart: the constellation drawn as a pixel-art star chart over deep space. Its stars are split into five part stages, travelled from the tail: **Stinger** (Shaula, kappa, iota), **Tail** (theta, eta, zeta), **Body** (mu, epsilon, tau), **Heart** (Antares, sigma) and **Claws** (Dschubba, beta, pi). A crown point above the figure is the **final stage, the full Scorpio**.

- **Part stages are their own maps:** each is a small "false constellation" shaped like that part (a `StarMap`). Its completion paints its own piece of the Scorpio, fitted to its stars (see Completion below). Winning one lights its stars and the strings between them on the chart, and its piece of the painted Scorpio joins the chart behind them, dormant (a few steps darker on the N ramp), forming from that part's stars on the chart the first time (#100, as a stage's painting does). The chart assembles the scorpion part by part; winning the final brings it to life: the whole figure flashes C0 twice and shows in full colour from then on. The **Stinger** is six stars laid out as the whole Scorpio's stinger: they come in from the right along the bottom of the sky, turn up at the left, then the telson runs right to the big star and the sting curls up from it. The first joint starts lit, leaving five to light (about 3.8 packs, all won).
- **The final** (stage 6) plays the full 14-star Scorpio (#61) as Orion's boss stage (below). It stays locked until every part is won; winning the fifth part unlocks it.
- **States:** a won part's stars are gold; the part to play next is warm, with a breathing ring round its point (its first star from the tail); locked parts are cool. Each part's point is its **main star**: a bigger 4-point star than the other chart stars and in its own tone, twinkling briefly on a beat of its own: icy blue while locked (the land ramp, unlike the chart's purple), white gold with diagonal glints once won. The point to play next is the biggest and flares brighter once a second, with longer, whiter arms and diagonal glints (solid steps, no fades). Numbers count the stages from the tail. The path is gold between won stars, warm through the part to play next, and a cool guide elsewhere. Tapping any star selects its part, and a comet travels there. Back from a win, the stage's point flashes as its stars light, then the comet travels to the stage it opened.
- **Living sky:** faint dust motes drift slowly up the milky way, about a third of the background stars twinkle (a brief M6 flash, each at its own time), and every 3 to 8 seconds a cool shooting star streaks down and sideways behind the chart.
- **Stage label:** no box: the selected stage's name sits between two thin rules tipped with little stars (warm when it can be played), with PLAY below in the game's button style (N0 fill, C2 border, C1 text).
- **Playing:** PLAY (REPLAY once won) opens the selected stage. Parts not built yet say "COMING SOON". In a stage, MAP (in the pause menu, and on the end screen) goes back to the chart, and the end screen names the map ("STINGER COMPLETE").
- **Progression:** a stage counts as won as soon as the core decides it, and is saved on the device (`user://progress.json`). The chart's progress is separate from the constellation built inside a stage. No upgrades, currencies or buffs.
- **Built so far:** every part (the Stinger, the Tail, the Body, the Heart and the Claws) and the final (the boss stage).

### Chapter 2: Aquarius (in progress)

The second chapter, part of the campaign: six stages built around the draining current kept in the trial ([chapter plan](chapter_2_3_plan.md)), all playable and painted. No Orion.

- **Chapters:** each is a `ChapterDef` (its figure, five part stages and final, crown point and art); `Chapter` keeps one chapter's progress, saved under its id beside the others. Every chapter has five parts and a final.
- **Opening:** Aquarius opens once Scorpio's final is won (Leo once Aquarius's is). The chart opens on the latest open chapter. The guided first run stays Scorpio's (the options' TUTORIAL plays Scorpio's Stinger from any chapter).
- **Going between chapters** (playtest: make it clean and easy): an arrow either side of the chart's heading leads to the chapter before and after, none past the ends (Scorpio, Aquarius, Leo; no wrapping). A chapter that's open shows a warm chevron (C2, C0 pressed, an N0 shadow; a 26 px tap target); one that's locked a padlock (an N8 shackle over an N7 body, an N3 keyhole). A tap on a chevron, or a swipe across the chart (at least 28 px, mostly sideways), is a **voyage across the sky** (playtest: travel from one constellation to another on one sky, not a slide): the chapters share one sky, the zodiac in the order the Sun passes it, a screen (180 px) per sign, each chart at its own sign's place (Scorpio, then Sagittarius, Capricornus, Aquarius, Pisces, Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo; forward, the next chapter, is to the right). The camera pans along it, speeding up over the first quarter, cruising, slowing over the last (0.6 s plus 0.28 s a sign: Scorpio to Aquarius 1.44 s, Aquarius to Leo 2.28 s), a whoosh as it sets off. The chart, its heading, numbers and stage label leave with the camera; the constellations between pass at its speed, drawn plainly as on a star map (an N8 cross with an M5 heart for each star, M6 glints with M5 arms for the brightest, solid N5 strings stopping short of them) with their names (3x5, N8) under them; the background stars drift behind at 0.4 of the camera's speed (half of them trailing a short N5/N4 streak while it's fast), the milky way and nebulae stay put; the new chart takes over halfway, when neither is on screen. The background stars are now one deterministic sky, placed per sign-wide section from its own seed, so they're the same at rest and on the way. The **selection star leads every voyage** (playtest: keep the selection's light star while travelling, even on a chapter that's all won): over the camera's speed-up it lifts off the stage that was selected and curves out to 46 px ahead of the screen's middle (towards where it's going), leads the cruise there with a speed trail streaming behind it as long as the camera is fast, and over the slow-down curves in to land on the new chapter's stage to play (with a chime), where the selection brackets show again; its trail follows the way it actually went. On an opening's voyage it's the comet that burst out past the arrow, and it lands on the new chapter's crown before the reveal. A tap on a padlock (or a swipe towards it) shakes it a pixel side to side, plays the refusal tone, and the subtitle says WIN THIS CHAPTER FIRST for 2.2 s. Taps wait while a slide, an unlock or an opening plays. Debug builds: [ and ] go to the chapter before or after, open or not; O plays the next chapter's opening.
- **Wide screens** (playtest: constellations popped in as a voyage began, and halfway when the chart changed): every chapter is drawn at its place on the sky all the time, an open one as its chart is (stars in their states, strings, paintings, crown, its heading), a locked one, or one a reveal is about to open, faint like the constellations between and named under it; the constellations between show whenever they're on screen. The stage label, PLAY and the numbers hide for the whole voyage and come back as the selection star lands.
- **The ground** (playtest: the chart's empty blue bottom looked odd): a low horizon along the chart's bottom edge (`assets/art/chart_horizon.png`, `tools/art/build_chart_horizon.py`, 36 px): a faint dithered glow, a far range with a lit edge, stepped pines and the grass bank over dark soil, the stage's land lower and simpler. It tiles every 180 px across a wide screen, stays put while the sky turns, and rises with the chart on the way down from the title; it keeps clear of PLAY.
- **A chapter opening** (playtest: satisfying and juicy): back on the chart from the first win of the final that opens the next chapter, once the figure has come to life, that chapter's padlock shakes, ever faster, as it heats (N7, C3, C2) inside a pulsing ring (0.6 s, a rattle); it bursts (a ring thrown out cooling C0 to C3, eight C0 rays, 18 shards falling away; the burst sound and a chime) and the chevron flashes in; a comet streaks from the crown out past the arrow, which bounces towards it, and off the screen (0.7 s). The comet leads the voyage on to the new chapter, which arrives empty: its stars pop in one every 0.07 s from its first part (a C0 cross and a ring cooling as it grows, each a rising note of the completion tune), each string once both its stars are in, the crown last, under a shower of 56 sparkles; its heading's title stamps C0 and a title card (the finals' style) stamps its name over CHAPTER n IS OPEN in the sky (C1, C2 rules, 2.4 s), the Sun's ignition sounding as the last star is in. Then a comet runs from the crown to its first stage, selected, ready to play. About 4.5 s in all; rare, so it can be big (no Big Bang signatures: no freeze, silence or black hole).
- **Its chart:** the full Aquarius, 14 stars spread at least 24 px apart, east to the left: the hand (epsilon) and shoulder (Sadalsuud) on the right, the head (Sadalmelik), the water jar's Y at the upper left (Sadachbia, zeta, eta, pi), the body (theta) down to the knee (lambda), the leg (tau, Skat) to the lower right, and the stream (phi, psi, 98) pouring down the left. Sizes follow brightness. Parts, travelled as the water goes: **Hand** (epsilon, Sadalsuud), **Body** (Sadalmelik, theta), **Legs** (lambda, tau, Skat), **Stream** (phi, psi, 98), then up to the **Jar** it pours from (Sadachbia, zeta, eta, pi), then the full **Aquarius** at the crown above the figure. (The Jar was third until the Legs and Stream were built first; the user chose to make it the last part.)
- **Stage 1, the Hand** (`StarMap.aquarius_hand`): an arm of five stars from the shoulder (upper right, lit) down to the hand (lower left), four to light. It teaches the current alone: a leftward flow over the sky's full height from x 72 to the right edge, **no drain**. Stars launched by the upper arm drift down toward the forearm and hand, which sit just past the flow where drifting stars come to rest. Gentle on purpose: the flow helps (spatial bots, 1,000 paired seeds: link-first 3.15 packs without it, 2.87 with it; every policy wins every run).
- **Stage 2, the Body** (`StarMap.aquarius_body`): the drain arrives and stays for the rest of the chapter. Flow over the full height from x 48, draining at that edge; the head (lit) at the upper right, the body falling through the flow to the knee at the edge, a hip branching right, the foot past the drain. Five to light. The current trial's layout; with stars flowing round obstacles (below): link-first 99.5% won (3.90 packs), aiming upstream 99.9% (3.75), launching every pack first 99.0% (4.43). A gentle introduction to the drain; later stages can raise the step.
- **Stage 3, the Legs** (`StarMap.aquarius_legs`, step 28): two legs branch from the hip (lit, upper right) at the knee. One reaches down and left, its shin a step from the drain at x 48 and its foot past it; the other stays upstream on the right (calf, and Skat as a big heel). Stars saved by the shin have little time; the right leg's drift across to the knee. Five to light. Spatial bots, 1,000 paired seeds: link-first 92.3% won (4.38 packs), aiming upstream 100% (3.84), launching every pack first 32.8%: hoarding is punished hard, aiming against the flow pays.
- **Stage 4, the Stream** (`StarMap.aquarius_stream`, step 32): the water **pours down** (`StarMap.current_direction`): the flow runs downward from x 16 to 163 and drains along its bottom edge at y 200, like a waterfall. The stream winds down from its source (lit, top left); its last star lies below the drain, above the ground. The water, the drain line, the aiming trails and the extinction all turn with it (the splash goes back up). Five to light. Link-first 98.5% won (3.71 packs), aiming upstream 100% (3.63), launch-first 99.0%: a breather after the Legs, since the waterfall also brings stars down onto the stream. Steps 36 and 48 barely changed it; the drain's height matters more.
- **Stage 5, the Jar** (`StarMap.aquarius_jar`, step 56): the hardest part, and a new twist, **the tide**: the flow reverses after every launch (left, then right; `StarMap.current_turns`) over the sky's full height from x 24 to 155, and drains at **both** sides. A star safe on one launch is carried out the other way on the next. The aim always previews the launch's own way (trails, brackets); the water flows the way the next launch will go, turning only once a launch has played out (its flight, burst and drift: playtest, it used to turn under stars still moving the old way); both drain lines show, the next launch's in S3 and the other dimmer (S2). A lost star splashes back the way it came. Its rule: BOTH SIDES DRAIN STARS / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST. The jar's Y stands in the middle (pi lit at the top, zeta its centre, eta and Sadachbia its arms), its lip below with the spout pouring down-left. Five to light. The step is a link's whole reach, so a star sloshes a full reach each launch: link-first 84.5% won (4.47 packs), launching every pack first 74.9%, aiming against the coming flow 100% (3.57). At step 32 the swings cancel out and it was trivial (100% for every policy).
- **The final, the rotating box** (`StarMap.aquarius_final`, step 56): the whole Aquarius (the chart's figure, hand and shoulder lit: twelve to light) in a box that drains on all four sides (x 20 to 159, y 96 to 227, inside the sky's margin so every side can drain); only pi, atop the jar, sits just above it. The flow turns a quarter after every launch: left, down, right, up. All four drain lines show, the next launch's in S3. Its rule: EVERY SIDE DRAINS / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST. Spatial bots, 300 paired seeds: link-first 71.7% won (8.13 packs), launching every pack first 51.0%, aiming against the coming flow 99.3% (7.46); without the flow every policy wins. At step 48: 88.0% / 99.7% / 77.7%; at 40 and below it was nearly always won. No boss presentation yet (Scorpio's is Orion's own).
- **Stars flow round what's in their way** (playtest: stars inside the flow often didn't move; a step blocked by another star or a landmark used to leave the star where it was, which stranded 86% of the Hand's in-flow stars): a star tries its full step, then the same step shifted across the flow by 4, 8, 12 or 16 px (nearest first), then each shorter step the same way; only a star with no room on its whole path stays (about 5% on the Hand, packed at the end of its flow; none on the Body). The aiming preview uses the same rule.
- **At-risk marks** (#149; playtest rule: a risk shows the moment it applies, since the player can't link while aiming and the aim's trails went unseen): while not aiming, every star the next launch drains wears a short ember arrow (S4) just past its downstream side, pointing where it goes: two dots and a chevron head, the whole arrow nudging a pixel that way every 0.6 s (a slow two-step, no flashing). It shows while the player links, so a star about to go can be linked out first; it hides while a launch plays out and gives way to the full trails and brackets while aiming. The core answers which (`RunState.launch_drains`): a star's destination is reserved before the burst, so it never depends on the aim; on the Jar and the final it answers for the way the next launch goes. The launch's own new stars can drain too; they're unknown until it bursts, so they're never marked, but while aiming the scatter ring shows where they would (#149: a fresh burst could splash out on the launch that brought it, unwarned): its dots inside the band the flow drains this launch turn ember (S4), the rest stay M5 (`RunState.burst_drains_at`). A Big Bang, drawn at random, clears the sky instead.
- **Water, not arrows** (playtest): faint streaks glide downstream through the field in whole pixels (about one per 900 px², 4-9 px long, 10-18 px/s, a fixed pattern), the leading pixel M5, then M4, the tail M3, under the stars.
- **A drained star goes out with a splash** (playtest: make it juicier, the sound punchier): it drifts to the drain and cuts out, and in 0.42 s of hard steps its white-hot core (C0/C1) pinches to a point, the line flares C2 then S4 over 21 px and narrows (13, then 5 px, S3), six ember sparks race up and down the line, and five water droplets (M5, then M4) splash back upstream and fall. Its sound is its own cue, `drain` (built in `tools/audio/build_sfx.py`): a hard thump, a gulp of water noise sinking fast, a whistle falling away (0.33 s, -7 dBFS).
- **No painting, no wait** (playtest: the end felt long): a stage whose painting isn't drawn yet ends its completion once the last string has rung (the song plus 0.5 s) instead of holding an empty sky for 3 s.
- **Each stage's own step:** `currents.stages` in balance.json, by map id (shipped: Hand 24, Body 24, Legs 28, Stream 32, Jar 56, final 56, as each stage above says); anything else uses `currents.step`.
- **Said before the first launch** (#128): as the player first aims in a run (before any pack is committed; a retry hears it again), the message line says the current's rule for 3.5 s, as what to do (#149, as Virgo's after its playtest: what the flow does, every draining stage naming the loss, then to link the stars its at-risk marks show first): THE FLOW CARRIES STARS / SO AIM UPSTREAM (Hand); THE FLOW DRAINS STARS / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST (Body, Legs, Stream); BOTH SIDES DRAIN STARS / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST (Jar); EVERY SIDE DRAINS / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST (final). **A run's first loss is said too** (#149: losses went unexplained; like Virgo's lone line): the first star a launch drains says THE FLOW TOOK A STAR / LINK MARKED ONES FIRST, once a run (an intro's demo drain doesn't count, and a loss while the stage's rule is still on show leaves it be: the next one says it). Leo's heat does the same for its first burn and its first fade. A launch made without aiming says it at its first move.
- **Shown as it arrives** (#148, like Leo's and Orion's intros): the Hand, Body, Stream, Jar and final open by showing their flow (`RunState.play_current_intro`); the Legs bring nothing new and open without one. A small, a medium and a big star stand upstream in a row along the flow (half a step, a step and a half and two and a half from the far edge, closer where the field is short), on the line across the flow furthest from the figure. Then the flow moves them twice as a launch would, without one: the water shows each way a beat before it flows (the Jar's tide and the final's box turn between the two), and a draining flow carries the nearest out with the full splash. What's left fades out. The rule is said as the stars appear. It pays nothing, uses no pack, doesn't turn the run's own flow and places its stars by rule, so packs and layout never shift (about 3 s).
- **Teaching lines are held** (#152): a stage's rule (current, heat, harvest), Virgo's lone-star warning and Orion's first-mark lines stay on the message line for at least 2.5 s. A launch, another message or a clear can't cut them in that time; only another teaching line can. A launcher only takes back a message it showed itself (LOAD A PLANET FIRST), so a player who launches right away still reads the rule.
- **Which way next** (#128): a turning flow (the Jar, the final) shows three small ember chevrons just inside the edge its next launch drains to, pointing at it; they turn with the water, once the launch has played out. The field and its drain feedback also stay on screen until a losing launch's drains have played (the core ends the run at once).

- **Pause:** in a stage the top-right button is a pause button (two bars). It opens PAUSED: RESUME, RESTART (a fresh run of the stage), the sound level, and MAP in a chapter. The stage holds still behind it, as under the table; a tap off the panel resumes. Like the other buttons it can't be pressed while a sequence plays.
- **Back (Android's back button, Escape):** goes back one level and never quits the game. An open menu closes (OPTIONS, PAUSED, the COMBOS table). In a stage with nothing open it opens PAUSED; on the end screen it goes back to the chart (as MAP; nothing outside a chapter). On the title or the chart with nothing open it does nothing (leaving the game is the phone's home gesture). Like the buttons, it's ignored while a sequence plays.
- **Leaving the game:** a stage pauses (PAUSED opens) when the game goes to the background or loses focus, unless it already holds still (PAUSED or the table) or the run is over; mid-sequence it opens once the sequence ends.
- Menus are plaques (N0, N6 border) of buttons in the game's button style (M1, C2 border, C1 text). No dimming behind them.

The Stinger's first play is guided. A refused action is refused like an invalid one (nothing is spent).

- **Show, don't tell (playtest):** what a link gives is pointed at as it lands (the dust counter, then the Sun), not explained on a text screen. The red planet is launched, not described: its twin bursts show the split and the big stars. The loaded planet is shown on its icon, in one short step (#148: two showing steps for the telescope's window and the icon's spin were 6 s of watching). **A full Sun is shown before the stage ends:** the guided run's Sun fills at `scorpio.tutorial_sun_target` (40) the first time, then at the stage's own (75). The scripted links give 25 (the sequence) + 5 (the small constellation star's three) and the red link at least 10 (a medium three; a big three gives 15, a sequence 25), so the red link always fills it. Design guess: the run starts with one red planet and the dust can't buy one by then (about 6 against 7), so the tutorial launches the owned one, and the buy step teaches buying a blue one once the planets run out.
- **Both ways to link, taught once:** the first link says either way works and acts the drag out; every link takes taps or drags (an earlier version made the first link tap-only and the second drag-only: it repeated itself).
- **Fewer refusals (research, Andersen et al. CHI 2012: locking the player into one action didn't help learning):** only the launches stay gated, since the scripted packs and the guided full Sun depend on them. A buy during the tutorial stays owned but doesn't switch the loaded planet; the planet's icon buys only a planet none is owned of, so a refused switch doesn't buy another.
- **The links are taught with the table** (#94), which the tutorial opens at its first link, before the hand plays. It replaces an earlier combo card (a plaque with a row of star art for each combo).
- Rules in the core (`Tutorial`, asked by `RunState`; each new step is a run event, `tutorial_step`), so the line and hand change once the animations have played.
- Reaching free play saves it (`user://progress.json`, `"tutorial": {"done": true}`): later Stinger runs aren't guided. A restart or MAP (from the pause menu) before then guides the next run again.
- **Retry:** once it's been finished, TUTORIAL in the options plays it again: Scorpio's Stinger, guided, its win counting as usual. PLAY on the Stinger stays unguided. There is no skip button.

### Final stage: Orion, the boss (stage 6, a prototype)

**Chapter identity:** Orion and his mark, volley, and hunting area belong exclusively to chapter 1, tied to Scorpio's mythology. They must not carry into later chapters or their prototypes, including as reskinned threats. Later chapters need their own mechanics and sources of pressure.

The **final** is the full Scorpio (`StarMap.final`: the 14 landmarks, the claw arc lit, so 11 to light) and the chapter's boss stage. Orion brings **every threat at once**, each with its shared tuning: the single mark, the volley and the hunting area, with no intros. It should feel like a boss: the fight is framed around him.

- **Unlock (chart):** back from the win that completes the fifth part, the Claws' point lights, then a comet flies from every part's main star into the crown (from the tail, a beat apart), rings close in on it, and it bursts: a ring thrown out to the screen's edge, eight rays, and every string flashing C0 for a beat. Then the final is selected and PLAY shows. While open and not yet won, the crown is the boss's point: bigger, flaring like the point to play next, and wearing an ember ring (S4) instead of the warm one. No sound on the chart yet.
- **Entrance:** the stage opens with `boss_appeared` (the core's event; it changes nothing in the run, and input waits for it). Orion's stars light one by one from his feet to his bow, then his whole figure flashes C0 and shakes a pixel (his roar) while a title card stamps onto the middle of the sky: "ORION" (C0 for a moment, then ember) over "THE HUNTER", between two ember rules. Sound: a low rumble, then a roar (existing cues, pitched).
- **The boss:** ember like on every stage (S2 lines, S3 bow, S4 stars), brighter while he hunts. A row of ember pips under his feet is his **health**: one per landmark still to light, 2x3 px (S4, its bottom row S3) 1 px apart, on a dark plate (N0) a pixel bigger than the row and rimmed in his lines' ember (S2), so it reads as a health bar (#149: bare 2 px pips read as a dashed line, no heavier than the strings); the plate shows from the roar as the pips fill. Each landmark lit hurts him: he flashes C0 and flinches a pixel, and a pip breaks (C0, then an empty N3 slot), with a low buzz. Presentation only: the health is the landmarks left.
- **Fall and reward:** the link that lights the last landmark clears the sky as usual; once the payouts land, Orion falls: one flash, then his stars burst one by one from the bow down to his feet, with the burst's sparks and sound. Then the strings sing (the usual tune), and **the painted Scorpio forms from its stars** (#100): it spreads outward from all 14 at once through the artwork, a bright edge leading (C0, then C1), until the figure is whole, flashes C0 once and stays. It holds the screen for a moment before the end screen, which says SCORPIO COMPLETE, the strings, and ORION DEFEATED, and sits on the bottom edge so the painting stays in view. Back on the chart, the assembled scorpion comes to life (two C0 flashes, then full colour).
- **Tuning:** the mark and hunting zone are shared with the Claws (`orion`, `hunt`); the volley is shared with the Body (`volley`).
- **Simulator (`--map final`):** now **39.6 / 43.0% won**, blue only / red when affordable, in 9.6 / 7.4 packs (the Balance snapshot), against the Claws' 76.2 / 95.8%. It doesn't model positions or playing around the threats, so it's pessimistic, but the final is clearly much harder than the Claws. When it was built (a four-star red planet, before the twin burst, #97 and #99): about 10.4 / 9.1 packs, 25.1% / 3.7% won (the Claws: 54.1% / 52.9%), and its what-ifs then: starting with the heart lit too (landmarks 0-4, 9 to light) 35.3% / 10.3%; the head down to epsilon lit (7 to light) 58.1% / 33.1%; no volley on the final 30.4% / 8.3%; `hunt.radius` 32 (shared) 45.0% / 8.2%. **Open question:** how hard the boss should be (the playtest decides).
- **Debug (chart, debug builds):** U previews every part won and plays the final's unlock; F previews the final won too and plays the painting forming. A preview shows on the chart only: it is never saved, never opens a stage, and opening any stage drops it.

### Orion's guided encounters (#93)

Playtest: a first-time player understood none of Orion's threats; each had an intro to watch and a line of text, which went unread. So the stage that introduces a threat also guides the player through it once, the first time it's played (saved with the progress, `"encounters": {"mark": true, ...}`; replays and later stages play normally). It **only guides**: nothing is held back, and the encounter ends on the player's first relevant action whatever it is. Its line is the only one saying the threat (#149: two lines saying one thing at once split the player's attention while the hand acts): the Tail's first mark doesn't also say LINK IT NEXT OR ORION SHOOTS above the launcher, and the Heart intro's caption (LAUNCH AND ORION SHOOTS HERE) comes down once the guide's line shows. Replays, the Claws and the final say them as before. Its line sits at the top of the sky like the tutorial's, centred to the right of Orion's corner (x 42 on); where that would cover a constellation star or, on the Body, the arrows hanging under the sky's top, it goes to the nearest spot lower down that keeps 3 px clear of them, tried centred then slid sideways (#149: the Body's line hid three of its five arrows and ran into the lit spine top; it now sits under the figure). It comes with the tutorial's hand (`RunState.encounter_step`, `Encounter`, the HUD's guide). While the mark's guide shows a link, the telescope stops aiming and doesn't aim again by itself, so following the hand picks stars (a tap on the telescope still aims); it aims again once the encounter is done.

- **The mark (the Tail):** after his first mark, the line says LINK THE MARKED STAR / OR HIS ARROW TAKES IT and the hand acts out a valid link that saves it (`RunState.encounter_link()`; with none to make, the hand points at the marked star). The next successful link ends it: saved, or the player sees the arrow take it.
- **The volley (the Body):** once the intro volley has played, the line says EVERY 2 LINKS / HIS ARROWS FALL (the interval from `volley.interval`) and the hand points down at the countdown above his head. The first real volley ends it, so the player sees it count down and fire on a real count.
- **The hunting circle (the Heart):** the intro leaves no circle, so it starts with the first real one: the line says LAUNCH AWAY FROM / HIS CIRCLE and the hand points at a spot to launch at that keeps the new stars out of it: every point the loaded pack bursts at (both of a red pack's) keeps its whole scatter ring (30 px) and 10 px more outside the circle (`RunState.is_safe_launch()`). The guide shows as the circle is marked, when the launch has just left the telescope empty, so the spot must keep clear for every planet, whichever the player picks next (#149: it used to be worked out for the blue, and a red's twin burst could land 22 px off it, in the circle). The spot is the first unlit constellation star that's safe, else the safe spot nearest the first unlit one, else the one furthest from the circle (`safe_launch_spot()`). The next launch ends it; one inside the circle shows the strike.
- **Not on the Claws or the final:** they bring threats already met.
- **Open:** the countdown's contrast is its own change (`art/volley-countdown-contrast`). Whether testers can now say what each threat does is for the playtest logs (#89).

### Tail stage: Orion the hunter (#64, a prototype)

The **Tail** is stage 2: six stars curling down the right of the sky and along the bottom (the first one starts lit, so five to light), with the top-left corner left to **Orion**, drawn there as an ember figure (S2 lines, S3 bow, S4 stars, like the boss; playtest: the dim cool figure went unseen) laid out like the real constellation (Meissa, Betelgeuse and Bellatrix, the slanted belt, Saiph and Rigel), with the Pi Orionis arc as his bow. He adds one twist to the stage:

- **The intro:** after launch 1's pack bursts, Orion marks one loose star in the sky: his figure and bow flash bright, a dotted ember sight line runs from his bow hand to the star, then an ember crosshair (unlike the warm selection ring and the landmarks' corner hints) closes on it and stays, jumping a pixel out and back once a second, while his bow stays a step brighter (drawn). The line above the launcher says "LINK IT NEXT OR ORION SHOOTS" (once per run).
- **The next link decides.** A successful link that uses the marked star saves it (it pays as usual). A successful link that leaves it behind pays as usual, then his figure lights up, his arrow flies to the marked star and breaks it: it pays nothing. Either way, once the link resolves, he marks a new loose star if any remain. The choice: build a combo that rescues the target, or sacrifice it for another combo. Launching packs to find a rescue is the cost.
- **Launches never fire.** A launch adds stars and keeps a standing mark; it marks a star only when none is marked (the intro, or after a Big Bang or a Sun clear emptied the sky). Invalid links, selecting, aiming, cancelled aims and purchases don't move Orion. Nothing marks before the intro launch. At most one mark at a time.
- **The bow warns before the release:** while the player traces a full, valid link that would leave the mark behind (and wouldn't clear the sky first), his figure and bow light up and the sight line holds from his bow to the target. Changing or cancelling the link stands the bow down. With two stars picked the target could still join, so the bow waits for the third.
- **Other ways a mark leaves:** a rekindled Sun clearing the sky, the completion, or a Big Bang take the mark with the star, and the arrow has nothing to hit. Orion marks loose sky stars only, never landmarks. There is no counterattack and no bonus.
- **Targets are random** (a seeded stream of their own, `run_seed ^ Orion.SEED_SALT`): some marks can't be saved (no combo for them in reach), and nothing quietly guarantees a rescue.
- **Win and loss:** after a link, the arrow flies before the check, so it can break the only combo left and lose the run; the new mark comes after the check (a mark never changes the sky), and only if the run goes on. A launch's check comes after its burst and mark.
- **Tuning:** `orion.first_mark_launch` (1) in `balance.json`. A map brings Orion (`StarMap.orion`), the balance says when he starts; without an `orion` block he never marks.
- **Simulator (`--map tail`):** the bots don't play around the mark; when a combo takes stars of its size they use the others first, so almost every combo sacrifices the target. Now (the Balance snapshot's runs), with Orion from launch 1: 100 / 100% won in 3.8 / 3.6 packs (blue only / red when affordable); `--orion-rescue` (the bots use the marked star first when a combo takes its size) 3.4 / 3.3; without Orion 3.3 / 3.2. So the mark costs about half a pack and never a run: a guided introduction with little risk. Before the twin burst: 4.4 / 4.2 packs, 99.7 / 99.9% won; rescue 3.9 / 3.8; from launch 2, 4.3 / 4.0; without Orion 3.8 / 3.7. The bots never launch to rescue or hold a combo back, so real play sits between the two.
- **Sound:** a low reject tone as he marks, a high whoosh as the arrow leaves, the burst sound as it hits (existing cues, pitched).

### Body stage: Orion's volley (#70, a prototype)

The **Body** is stage 3, unlocked by winning the Tail: nine stars, more connected than the Tail. A spine of five runs from the upper right down to the lower left, and its second and third stars each branch to a leg on either side, so two stars join four strings (eight strings in all). The spine's top star starts lit, so eight are left to light. Its painting is the plated body along the spine, with a jointed leg out through each leg star. Orion's figure keeps the top-left corner, and his twist here is the **volley**, which replaces the Tail's single mark:

- **The intro:** the stage opens with six random stars already in the sky, and after a beat Orion looses a volley that destroys them all, so the player sees what the volley does before playing. It pays nothing and doesn't count towards the next volley. Its stars come from the volley's own stream, so the packs and their layout are the same with or without it.
- **Every second successful link looses a volley.** The link resolves first (its combo pays, landmarks light, a full Sun rekindles and clears the sky). Then Orion's arrows destroy every loose sky star, for nothing. Landmarks and the constellation are never hit. An empty sky loses nothing. Either way the countdown starts again. (First playtest: half the sky every third link wasn't felt; now the whole sky every second link.)
- **What counts:** only successful links. Launches, purchases, aiming, invalid links and cancelled gestures don't advance the countdown, so launching stays the way to refill the sky. The link that completes the stage skips the volley (the run is won). A Sun rekindle clears the sky before the volley, so nothing is paid and destroyed twice.
- **No marks on the Body:** Orion doesn't mark single stars or fire the Tail's single shot here. The Claws (stage 5) combine the single mark and hunting area; the final adds the volley.
- **Countdown:** no number: a tiny constellation above Orion's head, one small star per link between volleys, joined by a dotted string, ember like Orion himself (#93, playtest: in dim blue N5/N6 it went unseen, as his figure had): an empty pip is a hollow S2 heart in S3 arms on an S2 string, a counted one burns S4 with S3 tips round a C0 heart. A counted link lights the next star (a white-hot heart with ember arms) and the string to it, hopping the row up two pixels and flashing white; on the last link the lit star glows between two embers (S4 / C3); when the volley fires every star lights, flashing and shaking a pixel side to side, stays ember while the arrows fly, then the row drops back in from above, unlit. His bow charges with it: drawn while the countdown runs, blinking bright on the last link. While the player traces a full link that would loose the volley, the bow holds steady and bright.
- **The volley, as falling arrows (#98):** each countdown starts with Orion shooting his volley up: the bow draws, five arrows fly straight up out of the sky 0.05 s apart, then drop into a row under its top (x 56-164, clear of his corner; an arrow that would hang within 3 px of a constellation star's halo slides along the row to the nearest clear spot, at most 13 px and 10 px from the next: on the final the fourth hangs 4 px left of lit beta, #149) and hang there pointing down, ember (S4 shafts, C3 heads), while the countdown runs; on the last link they shiver a pixel in step with the bow. Play waits while they fly up (about 0.8 s), at the stage's opening and after each volley. At zero they rain down: one arrow falls straight onto each victim from the top of the sky, 0.05 s apart (0.3 s each), and each victim bursts as its arrow lands, so every loss has a visible cause; in an empty sky the five arrows fall to the horizon. The countdown resets once the last arrow lands, and the next countdown's arrows go up. The intro volley falls the same way. The stage's completion takes the hanging arrows away (no volley follows). Presentation only: which stars are taken, the order against combos, other threats and the loss check, and the seeded stream are unchanged.
- **Randomness:** victims come from their own seeded stream (`run_seed ^ Volley.SEED_SALT`), so packs and layout never shift.
- **Win and loss:** the volley comes before the loss check, so it can break the last combo and lose the run.
- **Tuning:** `volley.interval` (2), `volley.fraction` (1.0) and `volley.intro_stars` (6, optional) in `balance.json`. A map brings the volley (`StarMap.volley`); without a `volley` block there is none.
- **Simulator (`--map body`):** the bots don't play around the volley (they never hold a combo back or launch first to spread the loss). Now (the Balance snapshot): **98.9 / 99.8% won** in 7.0 / 5.4 packs, blue only / red when affordable; without a volley 5.7 / 4.5 packs, all won. Before the twin burst, red often ran out of dust here: the whole sky every second link took 7.3 / 6.3 packs, 97.3% / 73.8% won; half the sky every third link (the first prototype), 6.4 / 5.7 (99.9% / 99.0%); without a volley, 6.1 / 5.4, all won. The bots link every combo at once, so the sky is usually thin when the volley lands; stars' positions and link reach aren't modelled, so real play can only do worse.
- **Sound:** the arrow's whoosh as the volley leaves, the burst sound as each star is hit (existing cues).

### Heart stage: Orion's hunting area (#71, a prototype)

The **Heart** is stage 4, unlocked by winning the Body: seven stars. A spine of five climbs from the lower left (towards the Body) through **Antares** (big, in the middle) and sigma to the upper right (towards the Claws), and Antares also branches up-left and down-right, so four strings meet at the heart (six strings). The lower-left star starts lit, so six are left to light. Its painting is the plated front of the body, widest at Antares, with a leg out through each branch star. Orion keeps the top-left corner, and his new twist here is the **hunting area**, which replaces the mark and the volley:

- **The intro:** like the Body's, the stage opens by playing the whole cycle once, so the player sees what the circle means and when it strikes. Three stars sit in the sky; after a beat Orion marks a circle round them: his figure flashes and a dotted ember sight line runs from his bow to it, then a dotted ember ring (S4: a midpoint circle, every third pixel, exactly symmetric) closes in with his marking tone, and the line above the launcher says "LAUNCH AND ORION SHOOTS HERE" (once per run). Then a demo planet of the loaded kind flies from the launcher into the circle and bursts; then his arrow strikes the circle and every star inside bursts, the demo's too. It pays nothing, uses no pack (the launcher keeps its planet) and leaves no circle. Its stars come from the hunt's own stream, so packs and layout are the same with or without it.
- **Marking:** after the first real launch's pack bursts, Orion marks a new circle the same way. The ring stays, jumping a pixel out and back once a second (the single mark's crosshair is four ticks; this is a ring). His bow stays a step brighter while it stands.
- **Every later launch strikes it.** The pack bursts first, then his arrow flies to the ring's centre and every loose sky star inside bursts as it lands, **the new pack's stars too**, for nothing. Then he marks a new circle. So before launching, the player links out the stars worth keeping from the ring, and aims the pack away from it.
- **What never strikes:** links (successful or not), purchases, aiming and cancelled gestures. They don't move the ring either; it waits for the next launch. Landmarks and the constellation are never hit. A Big Bang launch clears the sky first, so its strike finds nothing, and a new ring follows.
- **Placement:** a circle of `hunt.radius` px, wholly inside the sky and clear of Orion's figure, always **round a loose star** (playtest: a far-off circle wasn't a threat). He picks a loose star at random and the centre lands within the radius of it, so that star is always inside: of those centres, one covering the **fewest constellation stars still to light**, at random among them (#149: on the compact Heart and Claws a circle round a star near the figure lay over it, where the player launches to light it; now it threatens its star from the side away from the figure where it can). A star no such circle can reach (tucked beside his figure, or deep in a far corner of the sky) is passed over for another; with none left (an empty sky, as after a Big Bang or in the intro) the circle goes anywhere, the first of 64 tries covering none of them (else the one covering fewest). Drawn from its own seeded stream (`run_seed ^ Hunt.SEED_SALT`), so packs and layout never shift.
- **Win and loss:** the launch's loss check comes after the strike and the new ring, so a strike can break the last combo and lose the run. The stage is won by a link, never by a launch.
- **Spatial bots (#149, `tools/hunt/playtest.gd`):** bots playing through `RunState` (positions, the reach, where circles land and every loss check are the game's own; 1,000 paired seeds a stage, shipped balance). Naive links the best link (constellation stars first) before launching and aims at the next constellation star to light; careful also links out the marked star and the circle's stars first, and aims clear of the circle as near that star as it can. Won, naive / careful, with the circle placed anywhere round its star → covering the fewest constellation stars: Heart 50.6 / 85.9% → **81.0 / 95.6%**, Claws 48.8 / 82.8% → **79.3 / 94.5%**, final 0.1 / 7.0% → **4.4 / 17.2%**; circles over a constellation star still to light: 85-92% → 28-48%. Neither bot plays around the volley (they link at once), and on stages without the circle they score: Tail 100 / 100%, Body 76.6 / 76.6%. Simple bots, so this is pressure evidence: the final is much harder in space than the simulator's 40% says, and stays the playtest's question.
- **Tuning:** `hunt.radius` (38, reduced by 5% from 40 in #99) and `hunt.intro_stars` (3, optional; 0 = no intro) in `balance.json`. Size here means radius: 5% less radius gives 9.75% less circle area. The Heart, Claws and final share it; the warning ring and destruction boundary use the same value. A map brings the hunt (`StarMap.hunt`); without a `hunt` block there is none.
- **Radius reduction (#99):** simulator, seed 1, 10,000 runs per policy per stage, radius 40 → 38. Win rates, blue only / red when affordable: Heart 80.1% / 97.7% → 84.0% / 98.6%; Claws 63.7% / 82.9% → 68.4% / 85.7%; final 34.8% / 36.3% → 40.5% / 42.7%. Its estimated circle share falls from 19.6% to 17.7%. These are bot estimates: positions, link reach, aiming away and rescuing threatened stars are not modelled. Strike timing and target-selection rules are unchanged.
- **Simulator (`--map heart`):** the circle's prey (a random loose star when it's marked) is always taken if it's still there; where the other stars are isn't modelled, so each loose star (new ones too) is lost at the circle's share of the sky (17.7% at 38 px) on every launch from the second. Each sky star is tracked as itself, not by its size, so a prey linked out is not replaced by another star of its size, and each star is rolled once per strike. The bots never link the prey out first (a combo takes other stars of its size first) nor aim away, so this is pessimistic. Now (the Balance snapshot, radius 38): **83.8 / 99.0% won** in 5.3 / 4.3 packs, blue only / red when affordable. When it was built (radius 40, a four-star red): about 5.9 / 5.1 packs, 69.1% / 72.7% won (a circle anywhere: 89.9% / 89.1%; no hunt: 4.7 / 4.2, all won); other radii then: 24 px, 93.1% / 95.6%; 32 px, 84.4% / 87.9%; `--hunt-share 0.3`, 42.6% / 46.1%. (An earlier count, 52.2% / 52.5%, matched the prey by size and took a star of its size even after the prey itself was linked out.) The playtest decides the radius.
- **Sound:** the reject tone as he marks, the whoosh as the arrow leaves, the burst sound for each star it breaks (existing cues).

### Claws stage: single mark and hunting area (#74, #97)

The **Claws** are stage 5, unlocked by winning the Heart: seven stars. A neck climbs from the lower left (towards the Heart) to **Dschubba** (the head, big), which forks into two arms: one up to **beta**, one down to **pi** (six strings, 28-35 px), each bending back at its elbow as in the whole Scorpio. The neck's first star starts lit, so six are left to light. Its painting is the neck's plates, the carapace reaching back from Dschubba and an arm out to each claw star, ending in a heavy pincer that opens right, as the whole's do. Orion keeps the top-left corner with the **single mark (Tail) and hunting area (Heart)**, each with its shared tuning and seeded stream. The sky-wiping volley was removed from stage 5 in #97 to reduce frustration; it remains on the Body and final.

- **No intro.** Each threat was introduced on its own stage, so the Claws open on an empty sky (`StarMap.intros = false`). There is no volley countdown. The first launch's burst brings the ring, then the crosshair. The line above the launcher is the ring's ("LAUNCH AND ORION SHOOTS HERE"), once per run, as on the Heart; the mark's line isn't shown again.
- **After a successful link:** the combo pays (landmarks light; a full Sun rekindles and clears the sky), then the **single arrow** if the mark was left behind, then the **loss check**, then a **new mark** if the run goes on. A link never strikes or moves the circle.
- **After a launch:** the pack bursts (a Big Bang clears the sky first), then the **circle's strike** (the new pack's stars too), then a **new circle** round a loose star, then a **new mark** if none stands, then the loss check. A launch never shoots the single mark. There is no volley to count or fire on this stage.
- **A mark inside the circle:** the strike takes it like any star there (no reward). It wasn't "left behind": the next link's arrow can't shoot it, and Orion marks a new star on the same launch, from what the strike left. The circle doesn't avoid the mark, so a launch can be the way the mark is lost: link it out before launching to save it.
- **Independent threats:** links resolve the single mark; launches strike and replace the circle. Links never strike or move the circle. Neither action counts or fires a volley on the Claws.
- **No star is hit or paid twice:** each leaves once, by a combo, an arrow, the strike, a Sun clear or a Big Bang. Landmarks are never hit, marked or struck.
- **Completion:** the link that lights the last landmark clears the sky for nothing and wins; no arrow, mark or strike follows.
- **Tuning:** the shared blocks `orion.first_mark_launch` (1) and `hunt.radius` (38). `StarMap.claws().volley` is empty, matching the simulator's stage configuration.
- **Volley removal (#97):** simulator, seed 1, 10,000 runs per policy, shared radius 38: blue-only win rate 68.4% → 75.8% (5.6 → 5.5 packs per win); red when affordable 85.7% → 95.9% (4.8 → 4.6 packs). Positions, reach, aiming away and saving threatened stars are not modelled, so these are bot estimates. Body and final retain their volleys.
- **Historical simulator (before #97, `--map claws`):** the volley and the mark as on their stages; on a launch every star, a standing mark too, is struck once: for sure if it's the circle's prey, else at the circle's share of the sky. Stars are tracked as themselves, so a mark and a prey of the same size stay two stars. It doesn't model where stars are, the reach, aiming away, or linking a threatened star out first, so it's pessimistic. Blue only / red when affordable: about 6.2 / 5.4 packs, **54.0% / 53.2% won**. With `--orion-rescue` 59.4% / 59.0%. Radius 32: 70.3% / 70.0%; radius 24: 81.9% / 82.1%. The volley's tuning hardly moves it (interval 3: 54.9% / 55.9%; fraction 0.5: 54.2% / 54.4%): the bots link every combo at once, so its sky is mostly empty. The playtest decides keep, revise or abandon, and which threat to soften.
- **Historical bot playtest (before #97, the real core, then-shipped balance, 1000 seeds a stage):** bots playing through `RunState`, so positions, the reach, where circles land and the loss checks are the game's own. The naive bot links the best combo (landmarks first) and aims at the next landmark to light; the careful bot also links the mark and the circle's stars out first and aims off the circle. Won, naive / careful: Heart 14.2% / 75.1%, Body 89.6% / 89.6%, **Claws 8.5% / 61.0%** (about 5.5 packs a win). Almost every loss follows a strike (careful: 313 of 390 losses; volley 18, arrow 5, out of packs and dust 54). So the Claws are the hardest part stage but winnable, and playing around the circle and the mark is what wins. Verdict: **keep**, tuning unchanged; if human players find it too hard, soften the circle first (radius 32 or 24), not the volley.
- **Historical readability (before #97, Movie Maker recording, seed 8):** the three cues sit apart and read as different things: the countdown's two small crosses above Orion's head (one turns ember with a link left), the ember crosshair on the marked star, and the dotted ember ring in open sky (always clear of his figure). A link that leaves the mark behind lights his bow and holds the sight line on it; the arrow then takes it. The countdown is the faintest of the three cues.

### Scorpio constellation (prototype, #40)

An experiment to learn whether building a constellation makes the loop more fun and more challenging. It's on for playtesting (`scorpio.enabled` in `balance.json`); set it to `false` to play the plain stage.

- **Objective:** the constellation, not the Sun. Lighting every landmark wins.
- **The map:** the 14 stars of Scorpius's usual figure (#61) are the landmarks, spread out to at least 24 px apart so each can be picked, keeping the shape: the claw arc up on the right (beta, Dschubba, pi) branching from the head, sigma and Antares (the heart) to its left, a body falling steeply, and a tail that curls left along the bottom and hooks back up to the stinger (Shaula). Sizes follow the stars' brightness: Antares, Shaula and theta are big; Dschubba, beta, epsilon and kappa medium; the rest small. 13 strings join them (the head branches three ways). Each is small, medium or big. Unlit, it's drawn exactly like the sky star of its size, shape **and colour** (small orange, medium mauve, big blue-white), still and with no halo, so it reads as the sky star it stands in for (playtest: the medium one used to be lavender here but gold in the sky). Lit, it turns **gold** whatever its size, with a warm halo and a twinkle: gold is the colour of the lit strings and of nothing else in the sky, so a lit landmark can't be mistaken for a loose star (and, for colour-blind players to whom the small star's orange and the gold look alike, a lit landmark also wears a steady dotted C1 ring no sky star has, #150) (lit landmarks used to look like loose sky stars). The medium sky star was moved from gold to mauve to leave gold to them. Strings join neighbouring landmarks: dotted while waiting, lit when both ends are lit, with a small glint running along them. The claw arc (three landmarks, two strings) starts lit, so **11 landmarks and 11 strings** are left to build. An unlit landmark, which can be picked, also shows four small corner brackets in ember (C3/C4) that slowly swap: a cue that sky stars and lit landmarks never have. A corner within 2 px of Orion's crosshair on a marked star beside it is left out while the mark stands (#149: the crosshair's ticks ran into it and the two warm cues read as one shape); the other corners stay, so the landmark still reads as one to light.
- **Lighting a landmark:** an unlit landmark stands in for a star in a combo. Link it with two sky stars that make a valid combo with its size (tap or drag, any order). The combo pays as usual, the two sky stars are used up, and the landmark stays and lights up. When both ends of a string are lit, the string forms.
- **Link hint (playtest: show a new player what to pick next):** while a link is traced, the sky quiets around the stars that could come next and still make a valid combo, with every step in reach, so no extra marker is needed and a still frame reads. Those stars keep their normal sprite and halo; every 0.8 s, all in step, they shine: their glint frame with rays shooting 2 px then 1 px out of their four tips (C0 next to the star, C1 beyond), about a quarter-second, then rest (juice, not needed to read it). Every other star, unlit landmarks included, shows its "dim" frame (a step darker on its own size's colours) with no halo. The landmarks' corner brackets hide while tracing (the strings still show which stars belong), and the strings thin so the gold trace reads where it runs beside them: open strings keep one dot in four instead of one in two, built ones go dotted and hold still; the strings the link would form stay bright. A picked star keeps its dashed ring. After the first pick, the stars that stay bright are every star in its reach that some third star could finish; after the second, only the stars that finish it. Nothing changes before the first pick, and everything returns when the link ends. A landmark already in the link rules out a second one. The rule is `RunState.link_candidates()`; the views only draw it.
- **Playtest logging (#89, debug builds only):** to see where testers stall, what they try that gets refused and whether they read before moving on, each session appends one JSON object a line to `user://playtest/<timestamp>.jsonl` (on Windows `%APPDATA%/Godot/app_userdata/Dust In Space/playtest/`): `run_started` (stage, guided or not), `step_entered` / `step_left` (a tutorial step and its seconds), `refusal` (launch, link, buy or load, with the step and stage), `idle` (a gap of 2 s or more without a touch, logged at the touch that ends it) and `run_ended` (won, lost or left, packs launched, seconds). `python tools/playtest/summary.py <logs or folders>` prints, per step, the time spent (n, mean, median, max), the refusals by kind and the idle gaps. It only listens to signals (`PlaytestLog`, wired by Main) and adds no rules; release builds and headless runs (the tests, CI) write nothing.
- **Web playtest analytics:** friends play the web build, whose files stay in their browser. So every record (in any build) is also sent to the endpoint in `game/config/analytics.json` (empty in the repo: nothing is sent), meant for a Google Apps Script that appends one row a record to a sheet (`tools/playtest/apps_script.gs` has the setup). On the web it goes by `navigator.sendBeacon`, so a record logged as the tab closes still goes out; headless runs never send. Every record carries a random session id (one a launch of the game) and the run's time; `run_started` adds the platform (`web_android`, `web_ios`…); each collected link is a `link` record (combo, stars, dust, light); `run_ended` adds links, stars linked, links rejected, Big Bangs and dust earned. The sheet downloaded as CSV feeds `summary.py`, which adds a table per stage: runs, players, won / lost / left, win rate, and the median seconds, packs and stars linked. No personal data is sent.
- **Idle hint (#90, playtest: a player who freezes gets no help):** after `hints.idle_seconds` (4 s) with no interaction and a valid link in the sky, the guided run's pointing hand comes out and acts out dragging one valid link, exactly like the tutorial's first-link demo: it rests on the first star, slides to the next ones in an order that keeps every step in reach (the middle star reaches both ends), rests on the last, leaving its dotted C1 trail, and each star shines with the link hint's shine as the hand lands on it (nothing dims). The demo plays twice (about 4.4 s), then the hand goes and the wait starts over. Playtest: shining alone was too easy to miss; the hand shows both which stars and how to link them. On a constellation stage the link may hold one unlit landmark, never two. Any touch resets the wait (a pick, an aim, a buy, a tap anywhere) and stops a hint playing; nothing counts while a finger is down or a link is being traced, and the wait holds still while animations play (the event sequencer is busy: a burst, a Big Bang, the Sun igniting; or payouts are still flying), a hint playing stopping then. It plays in every run, guided or not, but holds while the tutorial's own hand is out (one hand at a time). **While the telescope aims (#152)** the hint never stops the aim, since the aim preview (drift trails, grow and burn outlines, reap outlines) may be what the player is reading: the hand acts out a launch instead, hovering 10 px above the reticle, pressing down onto it a pixel at a time, holding and lifting (about 1.4 s), twice, firing nothing and following the reticle if it moves. While an aim preview can show (a current, Leo's heat or Virgo's harvest) the wait before it is 8 s instead of 4 (presentation timing, a constant in `IdleHint`, not a balance value). Not aiming, the hint shows a link as above. Design guess: the telescope aims by itself at the start of a run and when a planet seats, so an idle player holding a planet now gets the launch hint, not a link; tapping the telescope to stop aiming brings the link hint back. Design guess: every valid link is as good as another, so the hint picks one from its own random stream (the run's randomness stays untouched; `RunState.idle_hint_link()`); the views only time and draw it (`IdleHint`, `HandDemo`).
- **Reach:** each step of a link, from one star to the next in the order they're picked, is at most `scorpio.max_link_distance` (56 px) long. The limit is per step, not for the whole link, so a link can still span two reaches, but not the whole map: packs have to be launched near the landmark to light. While tracing, the reach shows through the link hint (stars out of it dim; playtest: a dotted ring around the last star picked competed with the constellation lines), and the line to the finger strains toward it: from 70% of the reach it turns C2, from 85% C3 (ember). Past the reach the line stops at the limit, where an ember spark flickers, and runs on to the finger as sparse M5 dots. A star out of reach doesn't join: its step shakes in ember with the reject buzz, and the link so far stays. A link out of reach uses nothing up, and the loss check only counts combos that can be linked within reach. 56 px keeps every burst's own three stars linkable, and a landmark reachable when a pack is aimed within about 60 px of it.
- **One landmark per combo:** picking a second landmark in the same link is refused at once and the link is dropped. **The line says why (#91, playtest: the shake alone explained nothing):** the link line still shakes red like a wrong link's, and the message line above the launcher says ONE CONSTELLATION STAR / PER LINK (two lines, 132 px, so it fits the 180 px screen; the first line goes above the message line) for 2.5 s; the same refusal within 3 s says nothing new. Other refused picks (a step out of reach) keep their own shake and buzz and don't say it. The reason comes from the core (`RunState.pick_refusal()`); a visual example (one constellation star and two loose stars) is a possible follow-up if testers still miss the rule. A lit landmark can't be picked again.
- **Previews:** while tracing, the picked landmark keeps its colour and shows its halo and the selection ring, like a picked sky star (not gold, which would read as lit), and the strings the link would form are dashed.
- **A full Sun:** it fills at `scorpio.sun_target` (75 light, against 100 on the plain stage) and doesn't win here. It plays its ignition, then its light leaves as a sunbeam, a small comet flying from the Sun to the landmark it lights (the first unlit one next to a lit one), while the Sun still shines. The landmark lights as the beam lands, with a flash, a spreading ring and a burst's sparks; then every star left in the sky bursts, bottom to top, each paying `sun_dust_per_star` (1) dust that flies to the counter as it bursts, leaving a clean sky; then the Sun is back at 0 light. Light above the target is lost. The combo that lights the last landmark doesn't also rekindle the Sun. If the Sun lights the last landmark, that's the completion too, and it plays as usual.
- **Completion:** every sky star still on screen bursts, one after another from the bottom of the sky to the top, with a pack burst's sparks and sound; they pay nothing (the run is won), and the sky is left clean. Then, once every dust and light payout has landed, the constellation plays itself: string by string from the bottom of the sky to the top, each flashing and vibrating as it sounds the next note of a rising pentatonic tune. Then the map's **painting forms from the constellation's stars** (#100, playtest: it should come from the stars, not slide in from an edge): each opaque pixel appears once a radius growing from its nearest star reaches it, so the figure spreads outward from every star at once through the artwork around it, a bright edge leading (C0 at the front, C1 just inside), until it's whole; the art never moves, so it stays anchored to its stars. The radius is paced by area (at `k` of the 1.8 s, `k` of the painting has formed), since most of a figure lies close to its stars. Then it flashes C0 once and stays (playtest: no more line drawings): the whole Scorpio on the full maps, or, on a part stage, its own piece of the scorpion fitted to its stars (`StarMap.painting`, built by `tools/art/build_scorpio_figure.py`). It holds the screen for a moment, and the end screen sits on the bottom edge so the painting stays in view. The Sun doesn't ignite for this win. The end screen says SCORPIO COMPLETE and the strings formed.
- **Loss:** the usual check (no packs, no dust for one, no combo), where a combo may use one unlit landmark. A loss has a beat of its own before the end screen (#150): once the last payout lands, the stars left in the sky and the unlit landmarks dim a step (as the link hint dims them), the Sun sinks a step down its ramps, and every planet's buy plate gives one S4 refused nudge; 0.7 s later the end screen appears and says why: NO LINKS LEFT and NO DUST FOR A PLANET (from `RunState.loss_reasons()`).
- **Big Bang:** off on constellation stages (see Big Bang above). Forced (debug), it clears the sky stars as usual; lit landmarks stay lit.

Simulator (it doesn't model where stars are, so it ignores the reach): with the full 14-star Scorpius (11 landmarks to light), the Sun full at 75 and a rekindle clearing the sky, it's about 7.7 packs (blue only) or 6.8 (red when affordable), all won. With the earlier 8-landmark map (6 to light) it was 4.4 / 4.2, all won (4.5 / 4.2 at 50) (it was 3.9 / 3.8 when the rekindle paid 1 dust per sky star and left them in the sky); the rest of this paragraph is for the Sun at 100. With combos that light a landmark paying as usual, Scorpio is won in every run, in about 4.5 packs (blue only) or 4.1 (red when affordable), against about 88% / 87% in 8 / 6 packs on the plain stage. It's much easier; the playtest decides whether that's a problem. `--lighting-pays` shows what-ifs: half the dust and no light gives about 72%, dust only about 100%, light only about 17%, nothing about 3%. A one-off bot on the real core with real burst geometry (aiming each pack at the next landmark to light, up to 12 or 40 px off) also won every run: 4.3 packs with no reach and the Sun at 100; 4.1–4.2 with the 56 px reach and the Sun at 50 (4.3–4.4 at 48 px). The reach costs about 0.3 packs and the smaller Sun saves about 0.5, so Scorpio stays much easier than the plain stage.

### Sound

Sound effects only, no music. Soft chiptune: square and triangle voices, bell-like chimes for payouts. The sounds are synthesized by `tools/audio/build_sfx.py` into `assets/audio/`.

- Every interaction answers: pack load, buy and refused tap; a small two-note chime when a pack becomes buyable, together with its flash; slingshot pull (a creak per gem as it grows), cancel and launch; the tremble, then the burst; each star in a link rings up a triad; the link collects or buzzes.
- Payouts sound when the particles land, not when they're earned. Dust ticks climb a semitone per landing, like a slot count-up. Voice limits keep a stream of particles from piling up.
- Big Bang: it opens with the normal burst sound. The collapse draws in, then everything goes silent from the implosion to the bang, which is the loudest sound in the game.
- Sun ignition, then a win jingle or a falling loss phrase when the end screen appears, and a chime on restart. A restart cuts every sound and lifts the silence.
- The speaker in the top-left corner cycles on, low and mute, and is remembered between sessions; the options and the pause menu cycle the same level. Muted play keeps every visual.
- **Haptics (#150):** on phones, the moments that sell the slot machine by feel also vibrate, through the same cues (`Haptics.PATTERNS`): a tick for each star picked, a double tap for a valid link, a buzz for a refused link, step or buy, a thump for a burst or a drained star, a pulse for the Sun igniting, a long rumble for the Big Bang's bang, a rising triple for a win and one soft fall for a loss. They play even when the sound is muted (the point: muted phones are common), never in the Big Bang's silence, and never closer than 50 ms apart. Payout landings are heard, not felt. HAPTICS ON / OFF in the options; saved with the sound level.
- Menus: the title's tap rings the two-note chime and the launch whoosh, the star lands with the chart's select chime; the options open with the load cue, close with the cancel cue, tap with the select chime, a reset's hold trembles and its reset bursts low.
